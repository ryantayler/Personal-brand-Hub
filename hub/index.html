Headlines and card titles only. Uppercase, ultra condensed. Never for small labels, they turn cramped.

one handwritten moment per section

Caveat is the personal voice. Accent only, never body, never a headline, never two in one view. It takes the piece's lane colour. It is not the signature.

Card build spec · zero guesswork
Fill. One flat hex, no gradient. Pure black and pure white are never fills. Inner vignette. Colour fills only. Inset shadow, black at 38%, blur 25% of card width, spread 1.5%. Grey and panel cards stay flat. Outline. 1px solid. Full colour cards take their accent at 45% over base. Grey, panel, and split cards take the grey rim. Colour never rims a grey surface. Radius. 8px chips and swatches, 16px panels and utility, 24px hero and statement cards. Pills fully round. Edge accents are banned (ruling July 2026). Colour never marks a card by tinting one edge, no stripes down the left of a grey card, no accent bars on panels. Colour arrives as a full fill with an inner vignette, as accent text or a word pill, or as the 45% rim of a full colour card. Nothing in between. One accent per piece. Never two lanes in one frame. sound like you, every time
The voice

A working entrepreneur narrating real moves in the moment. We report what happened and let the reader draw the lesson. Never a content creator performing.

We are

Concrete. Specific. Casually Australian. Confident without selling. Relationship first, transaction second. Observant about craft, because experience is the job. Dry humour, deployed once.

We are not

A content creator, a guru, a hype man, a thought leader, a motivational account, or an employee talking about the day job. We never perform insight.

Voice constants · every format
1. First person, in the moment. Narrate from inside the event, not above it. 2. Concrete specifics beat clever framing. Welcome tea, QC testing, truss, LED. Specifics are the proof. 3. Contractions always. I'm, I've, could've. Formal constructions kill the register. 4. Real paragraphs. Two to four sentences each. Never one line per paragraph as a style. 5. Gary Provost rhythm. Short sentences. Medium sentences. And occasionally a long one that builds, listing the real details one after another, and lands with weight. 6. Casual Australian register. Plain talk. Mild profanity only when it is how a person would actually say it. 7. Close with one dry punch. Understated, earned by the story, then stop. Never chain two. 8. The lesson gets one or two sentences, placed where the story has already proven it.
Hard rules · never bend
No dashes of any kind in prose. Restructure with commas, full stops, or brackets. No colon in prose unless it introduces a list. Australian spelling always. Finalise, optimise, colour, organise. Never operator, CEO for hire, or executive. Entrepreneur, partner, growth partner, investor. Equity, acquisition, roll up, and PE never appear in public copy. Entrepreneur and partner carry the signal. Aaron Sansoni and ASG are backstory only. Past tense lesson framing, never present tense, never friction. No supplier names or deal specifics in China or sourcing content. Proof numbers are fixed. 115+ live events, 56,000+ attendees. Never mix with other figures. Named IP is never shoehorned. When a piece is genuinely in that territory, name it properly. Otherwise leave it out. Emoji. Never forced. One per post as a functional visual hook when it earns its place, like a country flag. Supplied formats are followed beat for beat. The format is the control group. If a rule conflicts, follow the format and flag it.
Ban list · instant rewrite if any appear
Staccato broetry. Rhetorical reversals and Not X, Y constructions. Analogies and metaphors as framing devices. Hype adjectives. Manufactured hooks. Guru imperatives. Chained punchlines at the close. Dramatic one line pivots. Borrowed business cliches. You do X, I do Y pitch constructions. Hustle narration. Internal brand vocabulary spoken aloud in copy. Temporal anchors in evergreen assets. Engagement bait questions. Hashtag blocks. Starting a post with Unpopular opinion. Allowed and different. A declared mission, one plain, concrete, checkable ambition sentence, is on brand.
LinkedIn post shape · the gold standard structure
1. Hook. One or two lines. A concrete situation stated plainly. It works because the situation is interesting, not because it is withheld. 2. Tension or contrast, brief. 3. The story, the longest section. Real details in flowing prose. The long Provost sentence usually lives here. 4. The principle, one short paragraph max, where positioning lives. 5. The punch, one line. Dry, confident, done. Roughly 120 to 220 words as a guide, not a hard rule. A small overrun never cuts a line that works.
Personal post shape · five beats, one paragraph each
1. The opening lesson line. Names that the lesson exists without stating it. The object must be small and physical. 2. The scene. Put us in the room. Clock, state of the room, who Ryan was at the time, the decision made. 3. The moment. It went wrong, or it clicked. Short and factual. 4. The lesson. The longest beat, where the POV lives. 5. The now. Proof he still carries it, closing on the punch. The final sentence is punch shaped, lands, stops. Approved moves. The borrowed credibility opener, roughly one Personal post in four. The callback close, the strongest structural move in the format.
Tone by format
LinkedIn. The partner room. Every post should leave a founder thinking this is how a partner operates. Instagram feed. Reuses the same copy as LinkedIn. One write, two platforms. Reels and stories. One or two punchy lines that lean on the visual. Story reshares get a three or four word reaction. Comments. Real reaction first, then one line of lived experience. Never lecture, never pitch. Slightly outside the lane is fine when it touches how Ryan works. Bios. Locked assets, reproduce exactly. Long bios are plain factual first person, evergreen, no pitching, no aphorisms.

One subject per piece. A problem an owner has, or a belief in the industry. Your proof picks the format, and the format walks you across the whole build.

75% solves the painful specific problems of founders in live events and production. 20% goes niche wide inside the expertise. 5% is personal, the human side that makes it real.

Step 2 · your proof picks the format

You do not choose the format by mood. The proof in your hand chooses it for you. Tap what you have.

Step 3 · read the build across

Formats tagged Yours are your strongest. Formats tagged Care fight your voice, use them rarely. Each card is the whole build, hook to Waterfall cuts.

Step 4 · before you record or post
The hook answers four things, fast
1. Who is it for. An owner knows in the first line this is for them. 2. Why you. The proof that earns the right to say it. 3. Where it is going. A reason to stay to the end. 4. One thing worth knowing. The single takeaway they leave with.
if the proof is real, it is good enough
The system around every piece
Waterfall everything. One long form pillar becomes many platform native cuts. Never write twenty things from scratch when one piece holds twenty. Long form is the trust engine. One ten minute piece builds the trust of roughly twenty shorts. Short form spreads awareness, long form compounds. One platform gets the innovation. LinkedIn is the partner room. Everything else coasts on Waterfall cuts until the priority is nailed. Reintroduce every time. New people always arrive. Re explain every framework, no insider references. Effort mix 70/20/10. 70% proven winners, 20% iterations, 10% big swings. Volume gathers data, iteration is the sauce. The scoreboard is partner conversations, never views. If a draft optimises for engagement at the cost of how a serious founder reads it, kill it.

The CRAFT Method™ · My Idea Crafting Framework™
Own · flagshipPublic
Own · your flagship IP
Your brain is for having ideas, not holding them.

A 5 step system turning your thoughts into ideas, and ideas into finished assets.

C · Capture · "don't trust your memory"
Quickly move a thought from Brain RAM™ into Digital RAM™ before it disappears.
R · Refine · "come back to it next week"
Go back to the thought and add to it, expand on it, refine it, and build it into a structured idea.
A · Assess · "ask someone who'll be honest"
Test the idea, run it through your mind, run it past friends, and prove it deserves to stay.
F · Finalise · "make it look like you mean it"
Visualise and document the idea, turn it into a finished asset, something tangible and public ready.
T · Transfer · "done means you share it"
Send the finished asset out into the world, then move it into Hard Storage™ and archive it in Digital RAM™.

The build standard · when the next framework graduates
A raw idea becomes ownable IP only when it clears all five gates. 1. Mechanism. A new way the thing works that is genuinely yours, not a borrowed shape with a fresh label. 2. Diagram. It can be drawn. If you cannot sketch it, it has no structure yet, only a slogan. 3. Sequence. Ordered steps with a reason the order is what it is. If the steps could shuffle with no loss, the sequence is decorative. 4. Contrarian POV. A built in belief that disagrees with the dominant narrative. Without an edge it is a checklist. 5. Slot in the system. A defined place beside the CRAFT Method™, the Momentum Formula, the Negotiation Playbook, and the CRAFT Consult. Orphans get parked, not shipped.

Every mentor framework (Hormozi, Ralston, Koe, Kallaway, Gray, Noske, Codie Sanchez). ASG proprietary frameworks including Human Signal, Triple Threat, Proof Bank, and Credibility Pattern. Teach them freely with attribution, never publish them as yours.

Derivative · needs a full rebuild first

INFINITE Framework, Consultation Framework V4, and Equity Consult. Raw material, not finished assets. Each needs its own mechanism, a diagrammable structure, and a contrarian edge before it can graduate to this shelf.

The two hard guardrails. Nothing is attributed to you unless it is verified yours. And a rebrand is not ownership, renaming someone else's framework does not make it yours. Ownership comes from a new mechanism, a diagram, a real sequence, a contrarian edge, and a slot in the system.
