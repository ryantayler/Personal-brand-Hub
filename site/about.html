The record

The whole career, in order

I've spent my career in live events and production. Straight out of uni I joined the top end of the Australian industry as a junior video editor, and five years later I was General Manager. I've delivered over 115 live events for more than 56,000 attendees, and I've managed multimillion dollar budgets.

Editing taught me the craft end of the business, how a show actually gets made and how much of the work never shows up on screen. Running a department taught me the other half, budgets, timelines, crew, venues, and the hundred decisions that sit between a plan and a room full of people.

In five years with Aaron Sansoni, I learned how to grow businesses. That's where the commercial side of my thinking came from, and it's the reason I look at an events business now and see the growth question rather than just the production question.

Teach · The belief

Most event businesses try to do everything themselves. Their own AV, their own crew, their own venue if they can swing it. It protects the margin and caps everything else, because nobody is the best at all of it. I believe you grow faster by partnering with people who are the best at their piece. You give up a clip of the ticket. You get back quality, reputation, and the time to be great at the thing you're actually great at.

In an emergency a supplier says no when it's inconvenient and a partner says yes to help you.

That's the difference the whole thesis rests on, and you only find out which one you've got on the day something breaks. Getting quotes is fine. Treating the relationship as the thing you can swap for a cheaper number is the part that costs you.
