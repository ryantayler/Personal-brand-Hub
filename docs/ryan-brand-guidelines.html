Brand guideline · v1.0 · one hero, two lanes
the whole system, on one page

One hero.
Two lanes.

Purple is you. Aqua is what you build. Pink is what you teach. One accent per piece, never more than one.

This is the reference for anyone representing the brand. Read it top to bottom and you will know which colour to reach for, which font to set, how to build a card, and what never to do. Dark is the real theme. Light is a supported adaptation for paper and bright screens. Design dark first, then check it holds on light using the toggle at the top.

Section · The meaning system · what each colour stands for
Three colours. One noun. Two verbs.

The brand runs on your two drivers in life, build and teach. Two loves, two lane colours. Purple sits above them as the hero. It is you, and everything you own.

Own
Purple · hero · the spine
You, and everything you own. Your IP, your frameworks, the equity thesis. The default when a piece has no clear verb. Purple is the noun.
Build
Aqua · lane one
Anything you make. Ventures, businesses, systems, live events and production. The first verb.
Teach
Pink · lane two
Anything you share to change how someone thinks. Content, point of view, hot takes, frameworks explained in public. The second verb.
The logic in one line. Purple is the noun (what you own and who you are). Aqua and pink are the two verbs (what you do with it). Build and teach are the two arms. Where they meet is your IP, and your IP is purple. The spine is what you own, and it never changes.
Which colour do I use? Is the piece about you or your owned IP? Purple. Are you making something? Aqua. Build. Are you sharing a lesson or a take? Pink. Teach. No clear verb? Default to purple. It stays rare, which keeps it premium.
Say the word, not just the colour. Colour alone is not enough. Some readers cannot tell the lanes apart, and colour drops out in greyscale and small sizes. So every coloured piece also carries its word: Own, Build, or Teach. The label is the meaning. The colour is the reinforcement.

Why aqua and pink behave differently on light. A colour's punch depends on its background. Aqua is very light, so on a white page it washes out, which is why it deepens to #0D9488. Pink sits at a middle brightness, so it holds its punch on white and the fill stays #FF2D6B. Purple is dark, so it holds on both themes with no change. That is why the hero never shifts.

Section · Card build spec · zero guesswork
Surfaces
The neutral surfaces for the active theme. Dark is canonical, light is the adaptation. Base #0A0A0A#F0F2F6 the page behind everything. Panel #111111#FFFFFF every card, panel and meta strip. One value, not two. Divider #1c1c1c#E6E9EF internal hairlines only. Rim #2E2E2E#DCE0E7 grey card outlines.
Fill
One flat hex, no gradient. Pure black and pure white are never used as a fill, only as text or as the base.
Inner vignette
A dark inset shadow that sinks the edges of a colour fill. It reads as depth, not as a light bloom. Colour #000000, opacity 38%, offset x0 y0. Blur = 25% of card width. Spread = 1.5% of card width. It scales with the card, so the ring keeps the same proportion at any size. It is not a fixed pixel value. Colour fills only. Grey and panel cards stay flat.
Vignette stacking
The shadow is inset, so it is clipped inside the border and never darkens the outline. All text sits above the vignette in the bright centre, so nothing loses legibility.
Outline
1px solid, fixed width. A card's rim follows its outer surface, so a rim never runs colour over grey. Full colour cards (the whole card is one accent, like the statement cards below) use the accent hex at 45% over the base. A tint, not the fill, so it reads as a lit rim. Split cards (colour swatch plus grey panel) and grey or panel cards take the grey rim around the whole container. On a split card the colour lives in the fill and vignette, not the rim. Internal hairlines (between rows, section tops) stay the divider grey, darker than any rim.
Outline solids
If a tool needs a flat hex instead of the 45% tint, these match on black: Purple #371867 · Aqua #1A6B5D · Pink #781A36
Radius
A committed card pair, one small utility value, nothing else. 16px default. Panels, utility and split cards. 24px large. The hero and full colour statement cards. 8px small. Chips, code chips and swatches. Toggles and pills are fully round. Radius is fixed and does not scale.
Spacing scale
Every gap, pad and margin snaps to one scale, in pixels. Border widths stay at 1px and sit outside it. No off scale values.

Text on fill
Fixed per accent, chosen by contrast, never by taste. Same on both themes. Purple fill. Off white text #ECE8E0. The only white text card. Aqua fill. Black text #0A0A0A. Pink fill. Black text #0A0A0A.
Derived tones
Each accent has two extra tones in the same hue, both flat, no vignette. Dark zone (near black tinted background, off white text): purple #161021 · aqua #10211E · pink #211015. Highlight (marking and emphasis, dark text): purple #DBCBF6 · aqua #C9F6EE · pink #F9CDDA.
One accent rule
One accent per piece. Never two lanes in the same frame. Purple is the default when a piece has no lane.

Section · Type · three faces, locked
Anton · headers
One hero.
Two lanes.
Your header face. Ultra condensed, heavy, uppercase. Maximum punch. Headlines and card titles only.
Inter · body and labels
The quick brown fox reads clean at every size, from paragraphs down to the smallest label.
All reading text and every small utility label. Weights 400, 500, 600. Never competes with the headline.
Anton for headers. Every headline and card title. Uppercase, ultra condensed, one face, no alternates.
Inter for body and small labels. Paragraphs, captions, eyebrows, tags, spec keys, hex values. Anton is condensed and turns cramped at small sizes, so anything small is set in Inter caps, not Anton. Weights loaded are 400 regular, 500 medium, 600 semibold.
Caveat for the handwritten accent. Your personal voice. One handwritten moment per section, never body, never headlines. It carries the lane colour of whatever you are speaking into. Full rules below.

Role. A third register beside Anton and Inter. The human touch. Accent only, never a headline, never body. One handwritten moment per view, never two. If the handwriting is everywhere, it is nowhere.
Allowed. A short belief line or pull quote, one sentence at most. A handwritten kicker sitting over a bold headline. Small captions or callouts. Through a long layout, one handwritten moment per section, spaced out, never stacked.
Banned. Body or paragraph copy. Headlines, which stay on Anton. Anything past one short line. More than one handwritten element in a single view. Your name as a sign off. The signature mark is the only sign off, always.
Colour is the bridge. The hand says it is you, personally. The colour says which lane you are speaking into. Purple is you and your IP. Aqua is a note on something you build. Pink is a belief you teach. Off white is a neutral aside. It is still one accent per piece. The hand takes the piece's lane colour, it never adds a second. Purple belongs here too. On the dark base the purple hand is set in purple, the same way the aqua and pink hands take their own colour. Web embed. Set family=Caveat:wght@400..700, then font-family:'Caveat',cursive.
Caveat is not your signature. Your signature is connected cursive. Caveat is a casual print hand. Cousins, not twins. Caveat never stands in for the signature mark.
Section · Signature · your sign off mark
Ink only. Black on light, off white on dark. The mark on any clean surface.
Signature rule (locked). Ink only, clean grounds only. Black on light, off white on dark. Never tinted, never on a colour fill, never on a neon, never on a busy photo. Colour is the handwriting font's job, not the signature's. The signature mark is the only sign off, always. Keep clear space around it of at least the height of the capital R. Flat, no vignette. If a hero piece needs a sign off, sign in ink on a clean panel beside it, or let Caveat carry the colour.
Section · Accessibility · why the values are what they are
Contrast targets. Reading text clears WCAG AA. That means a ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text and 3 to 1 for large headlines. Mute text is set to #8A8A8A on dark and #5F646B on light so secondary copy clears AA on both the panel and the base. The older grey sat on the fail line, so it was lightened. Text on fill is fixed by contrast: white on purple, black on aqua, black on pink. All clear AA on both themes. Accents as text shift by theme so labels stay legible. On light, aqua text deepens to #0A7A6E and pink text deepens to #C21E58. Purple is a brand colour and is used as text on both themes. On the dark base its contrast sits below the AA reading threshold, so it carries the most punch as display and accent type rather than long body copy, and where it carries meaning the label word carries it too. On light, hero purple #6D28D9 reads strongly as text and is used directly.
Purple display type. Hero purple as a large display word on the dark base is a deliberate brand accent. Where it carries meaning, that meaning is also written in the ink coloured words around it, so nothing is lost if the colour reads faint.
Never rely on colour alone. Roughly one in twelve men cannot separate the lanes by colour, and colour drops out in greyscale. That is why every coloured piece also carries its word: Own, Build, Teach.
Section · Status signals · state, not lane
Go. Caution. Stop.
A separate layer. Three colours that report a state, approved, warning, error. They are not lanes and never carry lane meaning. Because they live on their own layer, a signal can sit on a lane piece without breaking one accent per piece. A build card can still show a green tick. They are tuned a step calmer than the neon lanes, and amber is warm on purpose, so a signal never reads as brand voice.

Colour never alone. Same rule as the lanes. Every signal ships with an icon and a word, a tick for approved, an exclamation for warning, a cross for error. Colourblind readers and greyscale still get the message.
Text on the fill. On dark, black text on the solid. On light, the solid deepens and text flips to off white, so buttons and badges stay legible on paper. Banners use the zone tint with the standard ink colour, and the solid as the icon and the left edge.
Keep them small. Ticks, dots, borders, badges, banners. Never fill a whole content card with a signal colour. A signal marks a state, it does not become the piece.
Two guard rails, because two signals sit near a lane. Green is close to aqua and red is the closest of all to pink. Aqua is a cool teal and green is a warm grass. Pink is a magenta and red is a scarlet. To keep them apart in the eye: Never place an error control right beside a pink teach element. Give them air. Keep green out of build lane content. In a build piece, approval shows as a small tick or badge, not a green fill competing with the aqua.
Deprecated ideas (retired, kept for reference)
Cyan · retired Cyan was a fourth accent meaning events and production. It has been retired. Aqua now carries build, which includes events and production, so nothing is lost from the system. Why it went. Cyan sat too close to aqua in hue. The two were hard to tell apart, and they collapsed into one colour for readers who are colourblind or viewing in greyscale. Values, for reference. Fill #22D3EE (dark) and #0891B2 (deepened on light). Small label on light deepened to #0891B2. Outline. Tint rgba(34,211,238,.45), flat solid #156471. Tones. Dark zone #101E21, highlight #C9F1F7. Do not use cyan in new work. It lives here only so the history is not lost.
